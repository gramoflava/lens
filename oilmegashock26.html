What the video expects next

These are the conclusions and forecasts of the video author. Our arithmetic check is in grey.

Trucks×3

Trucks use approx. half of all diesel. If diesel costs 3 times more, truck transport costs 3 times more. Almost all goods travel by truck.

Check: diesel rose ×2.5, so ×2.5 is closer.
Food+20%

Transport is approx. 10% of the food price. Farm machines also use diesel. Thus food prices can increase by more than 20%.

Check: with ×2.5 the transport part gives approx. +15%.
Housing↑

Materials such as wood cost more to deliver. Construction machines use diesel. Houses become more expensive to build, buy and rent, and there will be fewer of them.

Flights+40%

Jet fuel has the same shortage. Airline fuel costs increased by 40%. The video expects tickets to cost 40% more, more cancellations and delays, and some airlines to go bankrupt.

Check: fuel is approx. 25–30% of airline costs, so approx. +10–15% on tickets.
Trade×3

Ships also use diesel-type fuel. Sea transport of goods costs approx. 3 times more. Exporters, factories that import parts, and countries that import cars or computers will have problems.

Loop↻

Oil itself travels by ship. Expensive fuel makes oil transport expensive. Thus a high oil price pushes the oil price higher.

Main conclusions

  • The crisis is not a forecast. It occurs now and is at its start.
  • Diesel affects daily life more than crude oil or gasoline, also for people who never buy diesel.
  • 10% less diesel and approx. ×2.5 price occur at the same time. This is the core of the crisis.
  • The longer the crisis continues, the larger and worse it becomes. It is not yet irreversible.
  • Advice: do not accept this as the new normal. Filling the tank in advance probably does not change much.

Signals to monitor

  • China. It buys crude at $135 and exports less fuel. The video thinks China expects a worse situation and makes reserves. China gives no reason.
  • US diesel export ban. It is under discussion. Economists say it will increase world prices, also US prices.
  • Crack spread and tanker rates. Both are at record levels and still increase.
  • Small-ship transfers off Oman. 6.5 M barrels per day depend on whether the Iranian navy detects them.
  • Fuel shortages. Some US filling stations had no fuel. One truck driver paid $1,000 for one tank. In Russia there are long queues for gasoline.

What the model shows

Reserves

Emergency reserves are not endless. When they run out, 2.5 M barrels per day more are missing. Without a deal, this is the next price step.

After a deal

Crude falls fast when Hormuz reopens, but not to the old level at once. Countries buy oil to refill their reserves for approx. one year.

Tankers first

Tanker rates fall first, because ships return in weeks. A short peak can occur when the waiting cargo moves at the same time.

Diesel last

Damaged refineries need months of repair. Attacks on Russian refineries do not depend on the Gulf. Thus diesel stays expensive longer than crude.

Adaptation

Over the years, people use less oil and other countries produce more. This lowers prices, also when nothing is solved.

Surplus risk

After a long crisis, new production and returning Gulf oil can give too much oil. Then crude can fall below the pre-war price.

How the model works
  • Crude. Gulf exports: 8 M now (UAE pipeline 1.5 + small ships 6.5), +5.5 M when the Saudi pipeline is repaired, 20 M approx. 3 months after Hormuz reopens. Minus reserves (2.5 M until they run out), China buying less (2.5 M now, 5.5 M if it stops stockpiling), and new supply from other countries (up to 3 M). Price = $68 × (1 − shortfall/100)−1/e. The elasticity e grows from 0.17 to approx. 0.32 as demand adapts. This gives $104 for today's −7 M.
  • Tankers. Hire rate = $45k × ek × tightness. Tightness comes from Hormuz (1.2), the Red Sea detour (1.0), lost or waiting tankers (0.5) and China's extra buying (0.3). Each part decays in 2–4 months after its problem ends. New ships slowly reduce tightness. Calibrated to $1.2 M per day today.
  • Diesel. Diesel price = (crude + $24 refining + extra shipping) × (1 − lost diesel/30)−1/e. Lost diesel: Russia 1.0, Gulf refineries 1.5, China 0.5. Repairs take 6–9 months after attacks stop. Crack spread = diesel − crude. Calibrated to $110 today.
